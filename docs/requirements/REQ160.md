## Description

A comment posted from a slide's composer is anchored to the slide its author was looking at while writing it, on every surface that draws the composer — the editor's strip under the canvas and the presenter's side panel. Changing the selected slide while a comment is half-written must not carry those words onto another slide's thread, and must not destroy them either.

Two properties, both required:

- **The draft is held per slide.** What is typed about one slide is offered back only on that slide; selecting another shows that slide's own draft, empty if it has none. Paging away parks the words rather than discarding them, and paging back restores them.
- **The write is addressed.** The composer names the slide it is drawing when it posts, rather than a caller-supplied closure resolving whichever slide is selected at submit time. A slow write clears the draft it came from, not the box the author has since moved to.

A failed write is likewise reported against the slide it was attempted on — a refusal left standing under the next slide's composer is the same defect stated in words.

## Notes

Filed from the code review of the canvas-first editor branch (REQ152–REQ158), which caught it on both call sites.

The defect: `SlideCommentsPanel` held `draft` as one `useState("")` that no slide change reset, while `CreatePage` and `PresenterPage` each passed `onSubmit={(body) => addComment(activeSlide.id, body)}` — a closure over the render's slide. Select slide 3, open the strip, type, click slide 4 in the rail, press Comment: the words survive, `activeSlide.id` is now slide 4's, and the comment is written to slide 4's thread with nothing on screen saying so. Most reachable on the presenter surface, where paging through slides with the panel open is the normal way to use it.

Parking the draft rather than dropping it is a deliberate choice between two silent outcomes: discarding would trade posting to the wrong slide for destroying a sentence somebody was still writing, on a stray click, with no warning. Keying it costs nothing and loses nothing. The drafts live exactly as long as the composer does — collapsing the strip or closing the presenter's panel drops them all, which is a dismissal the author asked for rather than a slip.

Keying the components on `activeSlide.id`, as the adjacent `PresenterNotesStrip` does, would fix the draft but collapse the strip under the author on every rail click — the notes strip can afford it because its content is a prop and it has no draft to lose. Holding the invariant inside the composer instead fixes both call sites at once and leaves no way for a third to reintroduce it.
