## Description

As a presenter on the presenter screen (`/present/<id>`) at any width below 1280px — a phone in my hand as a remote while the room screen runs on a laptop and projector, a tablet on a lectern, or a narrow laptop window — I want the top bar arranged for how I hold that screen and what I reach for in draft, live and ended. On a phone that means the controls I use while talking are in thumb reach, and at every width the controls look deliberately placed: one height and two clean edges. Then I am not hunting through a tidy-looking but wrongly prioritised bar.

## Notes

**Why this record exists.** The owner reviewed before/after screenshots of the bar that REQ189, REQ190 and REQ191 built (not yet merged at the time). The verdict: at 1280px and wider it is right and stays exactly as it is. Below that, on phones, tablets and the widths in between, it looks untidy and the controls do not look cleanly placed. The owner's hypothesis: the switches for chat and the other panels are probably not that important on a phone. This record challenges the layout below 1280px from who holds the screen and records the result. REQ189, REQ190 and REQ191 stay done; this record re-decides only the widths below 1280px.

**What is on screen today below 1280px** (`src/pages/PresenterPage.tsx` on this branch; every `:NNN` below is in that file unless named otherwise):

- **Shape.** The header (`:1003`) is a grid of three zones. The identity zone (`:1006`) holds Back, the divider and the title over a status line. The room console (`:1046`) holds the Door, Screen and Panels. The command zone (`:1139`) holds Deck, the lifecycle button and the app menu. Below `xl` the console takes a second line under the other two.
- **At 360px.** Line 1 is Back (bare chevron), the title over "Draft · 👥 0", the Deck trigger (icon only, `GalleryHorizontalEnd`), Start and the app menu (`⋯`). Line 2 is the Door (code, copy icon, QR, Share caret), Blank, and the Panels menu trigger. The slide rail follows as a row of chips.
- **At 768px and 1024px.** Line 1 is the same with labels ("Back", "Deck", "Start Presentation"). Line 2 is the Door with "Join:", Blank, and the five panel chips.
- **Paging.** Previous / position / Next is not in the bar. It is drawn under the slide (`slideNavigation`, `:792-821`, used at `:1400`), only for an editor of a live deck.
- **Panels on a phone.** An open panel (the `<aside>`s from `:1419`) renders below the slide, in the page flow below `md`.

**What makes it look untidy** (the diagnosis the decisions answer):

1. **Three heights in one row.** Pills are 38px, Deck and lifecycle 36px, and the app menu 34px. This is REQ192's defect, and it still holds on this branch at every width.
2. **A ragged second line.** Line 1 is anchored to both edges, but line 2 stops short of the right edge, so the two lines share only their left edge.
3. **Two menu buttons side by side on a phone.** One is an icon nobody can read (Deck), the other is `⋯`, and they compete with the lifecycle button between them for the narrowest row.
4. **The wrong things in reach on a phone.** Below 640px the one thing a presenter does over and over while talking is paging, and it is not in the bar at all. It sits under the slide, below the fold whenever the slide is tall: on the 360×740 screenshot the instruction slide alone runs down to about y=700, so anything drawn under a slide that tall starts below the fold. The bar it would belong with sits at the top edge, the hardest place to reach with the thumb of the hand holding the phone.

**Who holds the screen, and what they reach for.** The presenter screen is also the room screen (REQ191), but not at every width:

- **Below 640px: a phone used as a remote.** The room screen is a second browser on the same deck, a laptop driving the projector. What this phone does to the room reaches that screen by broadcast: the active slide, Blank (`sharedScreenView`), Start, End, the participation switch. Nothing renders the room screen below 640 CSS px — a 720p projector is 1280 CSS px wide — so below 640px this screen is the presenter's alone.
  - Draft: Start, the join link pasted into a chat, the code read aloud or the QR shown to the person next to them, Preview or Edit.
  - Live: Next and Previous over and over, Blank, the head-count, Presenter notes and the Q&A queue, End once.
  - Ended: Restart, Export, the results link, Who took part.
- **640–1279px: a tablet on a lectern, or a narrow laptop window.** It is sometimes mirrored to the projector, for example a tablet over HDMI or a laptop at 1024×768. This screen may therefore be the room's, and REQ191's reasoning holds here unchanged: the Door is the room's standing invitation and is drawn whole. Paging stays on the keyboard (arrow keys, a clicker) and under the slide, where the presenter is already looking.

**The owner's hypothesis, checked.** The five panel switches are not equally important on a phone:

- **Rarely reached from a phone:** Comments (the collaborators' thread on a slide, an authoring tool), Who took part (read after the session) and the Audience panel's channel switches.
- **Exactly what a remote is for:** Presenter notes and the Q&A queue.

Below 640px all five have been one Panels trigger since REQ190, so the hypothesis holds for the bar's face: no panel earns a place of its own there, and none gets one. It does not hold as a reason to withdraw them. Notes and Q&A are core on a phone, and dropping the other three would hide capabilities that are disabled-with-reason today (ADR-0025) for no width gained. The trigger moves into the dock instead. The problem the hypothesis was really pointing at is the second line's clutter and the phone's lack of a remote, and the decisions below answer that.

**Decisions (UI/UX concept stage, 2026-10-15) — build exactly this.** These were drawn as an interactive concept and measured at 360, 390, 414, 600, 768, 900, 1024 and 1279px wide (heights 740, 844, 896, 960, 1024, 1000, 768, 800). They cover draft, live and ended decks, an editor and a view-only viewer, and a 13- and a 55-character title: 96 combinations, all passing every check under *Done when*. Nothing below is an option. Everything REQ190 and REQ191 decided still holds unless a decision here says otherwise, and nothing changes at 1280px and wider.

*The idea.* The bar keeps its three zones at every width; what changes is where the console sits.

- **Below 640px the phone is a remote.** The top bar is one line: who and what, then the lifecycle button and one More menu. The room console docks to the bottom edge with paging, in thumb reach.
- **From 640 to 1279px the bar is two lines with two shared edges.** The Door sits under the title and the room switches sit under the command zone.
- **Every bordered or filled control has one height at each width:** 44px below 640, 40px from 640 to 1279.

*Decision 1 — three layouts by width.*

| Viewport | Top bar | Bottom dock |
|---|---|---|
| below `sm` (< 640px) | one line: Back (chevron) · title over status line · lifecycle (or View only) · More | one line: Door · Screen · Panels · Slides |
| `sm` to below `xl` (640–1279px) | line 1: Back · divider · title over status line · Deck · lifecycle · app menu; line 2: Door at the left edge, Screen and Panels at the right edge | none |
| `xl` and up | unchanged (REQ190/REQ191) | none |

The DOM order stays identity, console, command at every width, so the tab order is REQ190's. Below 640px the console comes before the command zone in tab order while it sits at the bottom of the screen. REQ190 accepted the same inversion below `xl`, and it is accepted again here, because reordering the DOM would move the ≥ 1280px bar.

*Decision 2 — below 640px the console is a bottom dock.*

- **One element, moved by CSS.** The console zone (`:1046`) keeps its classes and gains `data-bottom-dock` and `max-sm:fixed max-sm:inset-x-0 max-sm:bottom-0 max-sm:z-30 max-sm:gap-1.5 max-sm:border-t max-sm:border-border max-sm:bg-surface/95 max-sm:backdrop-blur-sm max-sm:px-3 max-sm:pt-2 max-sm:pb-[max(0.5rem,env(safe-area-inset-bottom))]`. No control is drawn twice for this.
- **The header's blur goes below `sm`.** The `<header>` (`:1003`) changes `backdrop-blur-sm` to `sm:backdrop-blur-sm`. A `backdrop-filter` makes the header the containing block of a `fixed` descendant, which would pin the dock to the header instead of the viewport. Below `sm` the header loses only its blur; its `bg-surface/80` stays.
- **Layers.** At `z-30` the dock is above the page and below every menu panel, the menu backdrop (`z-40`), the QR dialog and the toasts (`z-50`).
- **Room for the dock.** `src/index.css` gains `@media (width < 40rem) { :root:has([data-bottom-dock]) { --bottom-dock-height: calc(3.75rem + 1px + env(safe-area-inset-bottom)); } }`. That is the dock's 44px controls, its 8px padding above and below, and its 1px border. The page root (`:991`) gains `pb-[var(--bottom-dock-height,0px)]`, so the end of the page scrolls clear of the dock.
- **Toasts.** The container in `src/components/ui/Toast.tsx:38` changes `bottom-6` to `bottom-[calc(1.5rem+var(--bottom-dock-height,0px))]`, so a "Join link copied" toast never covers Next. The variable is unset on every other page, so nothing else moves.
- **Viewport meta.** `src/index.html`'s viewport meta stays as it is. Without `viewport-fit=cover`, `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` is `0` and the browser already keeps the page out of the unsafe area.
- **Contents, left to right in DOM order:**
  1. **Door.** See Decision 3.
  2. **Screen.** Blank, unchanged.
  3. **Panels.** The existing below-`sm` Panels menu trigger (`:1095-1135`), unchanged.
  4. **Slides.** New, below `sm` only: `<div role="group" aria-label="Slides" className={`${BAR_GROUP} ml-auto sm:hidden`}>` holding two `BAR_GROUP_ITEM`s with `min-w-10`, Previous (`ChevronLeft` 18) and Next (`ChevronRight` 18).
     - **Handlers.** `handleSlide(pres.activeSlideIndex - 1)` and `handleSlide(pres.activeSlideIndex + 1)`.
     - **Next's fill.** While enabled, Next adds `enabled:bg-primary enabled:text-on-primary enabled:hover:bg-primary-hover enabled:hover:text-on-primary`, the fill of today's in-slide Next. That keeps exactly one primary on screen in every state: Start in draft, Next in live (End is an outline), Restart in ended. A disabled Next is neutral.
     - **Disabled when** the viewer cannot edit the deck, when the deck is not live, or at the first or last slide. These are the conditions under which `slideNavigation` is not drawn or its buttons are disabled today. A view-only viewer and a draft or ended deck get the pair disabled, not hidden (ADR-0025), so the dock never changes shape.
     - **Names.** Each button's `aria-label` and `title` are the same string:
       - enabled: "Previous slide — slide N−1 of M" / "Next slide — slide N+1 of M";
       - live at either end: "Previous slide — this is the first slide" / "Next slide — this is the last slide";
       - draft: "… — start the presentation to page through it";
       - ended: "… — the session has ended; restart it to page through it";
       - a viewer without edit access: "… — you cannot edit this presentation, so its slides are not yours to move".
     - **One definition.** Derive these once, as a small `slidePaging` value (each direction's `disabled` and label), and have both the dock and `slideNavigation` read it (ADR-0027), so the two cannot come to disagree.
- **Paging under the slide.** Below 640px, the deck view's in-slide paging row is placed in the dock instead of under the slide. At `:1400`, wrap it: `<div className="max-sm:hidden">{slideNavigation}</div>`. The blanked screen (`:844-877`) has no bar and so no dock, and keeps drawing `slideNavigation` unchanged. The visible "N / M" is not repeated in the dock: the rail's highlighted chip shows the position, and the Previous and Next names state it.
- **Measured.** The dock is 61px tall at every phone width.
  - At 360px its four groups measure 104, 44, 58 and 88px, leaving 24px of the 336px line spare.
  - With all five panels open, the Panels trigger grows to 71px for its count, and 10px stay spare.
  - Nothing else in the dock changes width: the count is one digit (there are five panels) and the join code is always six digits.
  - Menus opened from the dock flip above it via `appMenuPlacement()` and stayed inside the viewport in every combination.

- **The Join menu.**
  - Its heading is "Join · <code>".
  - Its rows, in order:
    1. "Show QR code": the descriptor's `qr` action. It closes the menu first, as the edit link does.
    2. "Copy join code": `join.onClick`. It shows `Check` and "Copied!" while `join.copied`, and stays open.
    3. Then the Share menu's rows unchanged: Copy join link, Copy embed code, and, after a divider, the edit link for a holder of the edit token.

", the embed code or the edit link" : " or the embed code"}`. Its `title` is `Join ${code}`. The visible code sits inside the name, which WCAG 2.5.3 (label in name) requires.
- **Overturned below 640px only: REQ191's one-tap QR and one-tap copy.** Both are now two taps. The reason is that below 640px this screen is not the room's (see *Who holds the screen*):

*Decision 4 — below 640px the top line is Back, title, lifecycle and one More menu.*

- **Back.** Back (`:1007`) gains `max-sm:size-11 max-sm:justify-center max-sm:-ml-3.5 max-sm:-mr-1.5`. That is a 44px target, with the chevron still optically on the 16px page margin.
- **The Deck trigger.** It gains `max-sm:hidden`.
- **The app menu.** The plain `<AppMenu/>` at `:1274` gains a `className` of `max-sm:hidden max-xl:size-10` (see Decision 6).
- **More.** A second `<AppMenu/>`, `sm:hidden`, carries the Deck rows as a leading section. Its trigger's `aria-label` is `More — ${deckEditable ? "edit, " : ""}preview, share results, export, reset, appearance and legal` and its `title` is "More".
- **Its panel, top to bottom:**
  1. the heading "Deck";
  2. the Deck menu's rows exactly as today (Edit presentation for an editor of a draft or ended deck, Preview, Share results…, Export…, Reset results…, with their names, dividers, disabled reasons and close-first rule);
  3. a `MENU_DIVIDER`;
  4. the app menu's own Appearance, feedback and Legal sections, unchanged.
- **One definition of the rows.** Hoist the rows into one `deckMenuRows(close)` render function in `PresenterPage`, and have both the Deck `BarMenu` and More call it, so the two cannot come to differ (ADR-0027).
- **`AppMenu` gains two optional props** (`src/components/ui/AppMenu.tsx`), and without them it renders and behaves exactly as today on every surface (`JoinPage`, `CreatePage`, `TemplatesPage`, the blanked presenter screen):
  - `className?: string`, appended to the trigger's classes.
  - `leading?: { label: string; title: string; heading: string; rows: (close: () => void) => ReactNode }`. When it is given:
    - the trigger takes `label` and `title` instead of its own name;
    - the panel is `w-64` and starts with `heading` and `rows(close)`, then a `MENU_DIVIDER`;
    - focus on open goes to the first `[data-bar-menu-row]:not(:disabled)`, falling back to the chosen theme radio when every row is disabled, as for a view-only viewer;
    - the panel's Tab rule includes `[data-bar-menu-row]:not(:disabled)`.
  - `AppMenu` keeps owning its panel, the theme state and the feedback dialog, so the feedback row still works from More.
- **Why merge.** On a phone the Deck icon and `⋯` are two unlabelled menus side by side, and only the lifecycle button between them is really primary. One menu buys the title 44px and removes a decision the presenter cannot make at a glance.
- **Scope of placement.** Deck actions and app-wide settings in one panel are kept apart by headings ("Deck", "Appearance", "Legal"), so each section's scope stays readable (ADR-0031).
- **From 640px.** The Deck trigger (labelled) and the app menu stay separate as today.
- **Lifecycle.** Unchanged in what it says and does. It is 44px tall below `sm` (Decision 6), and End stays at the top, away from the thumb's resting place.

*Decision 5 — from 640 to 1279px, line 2 holds both edges.*

- **The rule.** The Screen group (`:1061`) gains `sm:max-xl:ml-auto`. The Door then sits flush with Back's left edge, and Screen and Panels sit flush with the app menu's right edge.
- **Measured.** Both edges match to the pixel at 640, 768, 1024 and 1279px. At 640px with a 55-character title the title keeps 201px in the widest case (a draft deck with "Start Presentation"). At 768px the widest case keeps 329px.
- **Nothing else changes in this range.** The order, labels, the Door, the five panel chips and the status line are as REQ190/REQ191 drew them.

*Decision 6 — one control height per width (REQ192 folded in).*

| Viewport | Height of every bordered or filled control | How |
|---|---|---|
| below 640px | 44px, top line and dock alike | `BAR_GROUP_ITEM` gains `max-sm:h-9.5 max-sm:min-w-9.5`: a 38px item plus the pill's 2px padding and 1px border on each side. The lifecycle buttons gain `max-sm:h-11`. More gets `max-sm:size-11` in its `className`. |
| 640–1279px | 40px | `BAR_GROUP_ITEM` gains `max-xl:h-8.5 max-xl:min-w-8.5` (34px items). The Deck trigger gains `max-xl:h-10`. The lifecycle buttons gain `max-xl:h-10`. The app menu gets `max-xl:size-10` in its `className`. |
| 1280px and up | unchanged: 38 / 36 / 34px | — |

- **Where the heights live.** They go into the shared tokens in `ShareCluster.tsx`, not per site (ADR-0028).
- **Variant order.** The breakpoint variants sort after the base `h-8`/`h-9`/`size-8.5`, so nothing at `xl` changes. `max-sm:` wins over `max-xl:` below 640px because it is the narrower range and Tailwind emits it later. Each lifecycle button keeps `h-9` and adds both variants.
- **REQ192, below 1280px.** Met: every bordered or filled control has one rendered height per width, measured in all 96 combinations (pills counted as one control, as REQ192 says). Touch targets are at least 38px below 640 and 34px from 640 to 1279, above WCAG 2.5.8's 24px.
- **REQ192, at 1280px and wider.** *Amended 2026-10-15 — see "Owner amendment" after Decision 7: the owner has since taken this up, and every control there is now 38px.* As decided at the concept stage: not met, and not touched here. The running app renders the pills at 38px, Deck and the lifecycle button at 36px and the app menu at 34px at both 1280 and 1536px. Making them one height would change the ≥ 1280px bar, which this record holds pixel-identical by the owner's decision. REQ192 therefore stays open for those widths, as an owner decision between REQ192 and "keep desktop exactly as it is". *Decision 7 — below 640px an opened panel is a sheet above the dock (2026-10-15, follow-up 1).*

- **The defect.** Below `sm` the panels beside the slide (`<aside>`s for notes, comments, Q&A, audience and who took part) rendered in the page flow under the slide. Opening one from the dock's Panels menu changed nothing where the presenter was looking.
- **The smallest design that fixes it.** The five `<aside>`s get one wrapper. From `sm` it is `sm:contents`, which generates no box, so the panels lay out beside or under the slide exactly as before. Below `sm` it is a sheet:
  - fixed above the dock (`max-sm:fixed max-sm:inset-x-0 max-sm:bottom-[var(--bottom-dock-height,0px)] max-sm:z-20`), at most `55dvh` tall, scrolling inside itself (`max-sm:overflow-y-auto max-sm:overscroll-contain`);
  - drawn only while a panel is (`max-sm:hidden max-sm:has-[aside]:flex`), with no state of its own;
  - the open panels stack in it in their existing order, and several may still be open at once (REQ190);
  - each panel scrolls itself into the sheet's view when it is drawn (`revealInPanelSheet`, a ref that does nothing from `sm`), so the panel just opened is the one in sight;
  - a sticky header (`sm:hidden`) carries an `<h2>` "Panels", so heading navigation reaches the sheet, which sits after the slide in the tab order, and a "Hide panels" close that closes every open panel through the panel descriptor and hands focus back to the dock's Panels trigger. One panel at a time is still closed from the dock's Panels menu, just below. The wrapper takes no landmark role: it is `sm:contents` from `sm`, where the role would carry over to the desktop layout.
- **At `z-20`** the sheet is above the page and below the dock (`z-30`), every menu, the QR dialog and the toasts. It covers the lower part of the slide while it is open, which is what a sheet does. The dock's menus open over it.
- **Measured** at 360, 390, 414 and 600px for a live deck as its editor, opening each panel from the dock:
  - the sheet's bottom edge is the dock's top edge;
  - each panel's heading is in view and hit-tests as the panel's own, without the page or the sheet being scrolled;
  - "Hide panels" leaves no panel and focus on the Panels trigger.
  - With notes, Q&A, audience and who took part opened in that order, the last one is in view.
  - Comments was checked as the signed-in owner of a deck, the only viewer who may open it.
- **Focus behind the sheet: Escape takes it down.** A control on the page that focus reaches while the sheet is open can sit wholly behind it (WCAG 2.4.11 — with Q&A open at 360px, the slide's "Close this slide", "Count", "Percent" and "Both" do), and a keyboard user who zooms gets this layout from a desktop window. The root's `scroll-padding-bottom` covers the dock alone, because the sheet's height varies with its contents, so the sheet is not scrolled clear of. Instead, while the sheet is up, Escape closes every open panel (`panelSheetEscape` in `PresenterPage.tsx`):
  - pressed outside the sheet, focus stays where it is, on the control the sheet was covering, which is now in sight;
  - pressed inside the sheet, focus goes to the dock's Panels trigger, as from "Hide panels", since what held it goes with the sheet;
  - an Escape a menu has answered is not also the sheet's: `useCloseOnEscape` marks it handled, and a menu panel keeps its keys to itself; the QR overlay and the Reset, share-edit, export and results-link dialogs own theirs too;
  - an Escape typed in a field inside the sheet — the chat's draft, a note — is the field's: the sheet stays up and the draft with it, since a field in the sheet is never covered by it. A field behind the sheet still takes it down, and an Escape while an input method is composing is never the sheet's (`keyBelongsToField` in `PresenterPage.tsx`). The same check keeps Space and the arrow keys typed into any field from paging the deck.
  The alternative, publishing the sheet's measured height into the page's end padding and `scroll-padding-bottom`, is more code for the same outcome.

*Roads not taken.*

- **Drop Comments, Who took part and Audience on a phone.** This was the owner's hypothesis taken literally. It gains no width, because they are already one trigger. It would hide capabilities that are disabled-with-reason today, and it would make the phone a second-class presenter.
- **Keep the console at the top on a phone and only even out the heights.** That would be tidier and still leave the remote's one repeated action out of reach.
- **Paging in the top bar on a phone.** It is the hardest place to reach with the hand holding the phone.
- **The dock from 640 to 1279px too.** A lectern tablet and a laptop window are not held in one hand, and this range may be the room screen, where a bar at the bottom would cover the slide the room is reading.
- **One line from 1024px.** Computed from the zone widths measured in the concept (not drawn): at 1024px a one-line bar leaves the title about 150px even with "Start" and an icon-only Deck. That fits a short title only, and the gain is one line of a 768px-high screen.
- **A separate "remote" route.** That would mean a second surface to keep in step. The same page with the console docked is the same controls, the same names and the same disabled reasons.
