## Description

A question's results can be broken down by the answers the same participants gave to an earlier slide on the deck, joined on participant id.

## Updates

- 2026-08-14 — Segmentation shipped: server/segmentation.ts joins a slide's respondents to an earlier slide's answers on participant id (one group per authored option, empty ones kept, plus an explicit group for people who did not answer the grouping slide), and getSegmentedResults feeds each group through the ordinary aggregateSlideResults over a participant-scoped ResultsSource — so a segment's numbers are the same aggregation, never a second one. GET /presentations/:id/results/:slideId/segments?by= serves it behind both slides' reveal gates, with a small-cell floor (5) that only an editor reads past. Grouping is limited to a single-select choice or a select-answer quiz slide earlier in the deck; every other pairing is a 400 with a refused code and the reason in words.
- 2026-08-14 — Review NST9136 found the small-cell floor defeated by subtraction: suppression was decided one group at a time, so a lone group below the floor was recoverable as the unsegmented tally minus the published groups — an anonymous caller could read one participant's answers. Fixed by deciding disclosure over the whole set (segmentDisclosure): a group is never held back alone, the smallest group clearing the floor is held back with it, empty groups do not count toward the pair, and a held-back group now publishes no head count either. Two further limits followed: a slide whose tally lists one entry per respondent (open-text, word cloud, pin, grid, guess, leaderboard, typed quiz) is not broken down for a non-editor at all, since matching entries across two breakdowns isolates a person whatever the group sizes; and a slide with no tally is refused as a target. The reviewer's repro is now a regression test.
