## Description

A deck can filter free-text submissions against a word list selected per language. Matched content is withheld from the audience view while the raw value stays reachable on the results surface.

## Notes

Defaults this requirement left open, as built:

- **A per-deck setting, off by default.** `profanityFilterEnabled` (default `false`) and `profanityFilterLanguage` (default `null`) are authored with the deck — create, PATCH (gated like every mutation: owner, edit token or `edit` grant) and the editor's Presentation settings, beside the reveal mode — and default the same way on every schema, so a deck stored before them reads off with no migration. With the filter off nothing changes: every payload carries the answers as before, plus two stated keys, `filteredCount: null` and `filteredAnswers: null`, and the workbook's `Filtered` column stays empty.
- **Word lists for all seven participant languages, shipped in the repository.** `server/profanity-words/<language>.txt` (en, de, fr, es, it, pt, nl) are imported as text, so the server bundle inlines them; nothing is read from disk or fetched at runtime, and nothing under `src/` imports them, so no browser bundle carries one. They are original compilations written for this repository rather than a third-party list: obscene words, slurs and strong personal insults, plus the English swearwords speakers of the six other languages use as loanwords. Mild interjections and any word whose everyday meaning is innocent once case and accents are folded are kept off — each list's header names the look-alikes it leaves alone (a bird, a cone, Troy, Moses, a disease name), and `server/profanity-filter.schemas.test.ts` asserts every one of them passes, so an entry added later that collides fails a test rather than a room. Those tests also hold the innocent phrases a review of this requirement found withheld — "a chink in the armour", "pussy willow", "going out for a fag", "to retard growth", "honky-tonk", "o puto da escola", "la zorra y el cuervo", "pain bâtard" — whose words came off the lists. They are known cases, not a proof that no other innocent phrase collides. Diacritic-insensitivity has a price that shows here: Spanish "coño" folds onto "cono" (a cone) and is therefore not on the list.
- **Which list.** `profanityFilterLanguage: null` follows the deck's `language`, read when asked (so re-authoring a deck into German moves the filter with it) as its primary subtag, English for a tag the participant screens do not speak — `participantLanguageFor()`, the one reading, which `resolveLang` delegates to for a phone's dictionary. A list picked by name stands whatever the deck's language does. One list per deck; there are no custom per-deck lists.
- **How it matches.** Server-side, whole words, case- and accent-insensitive: NFKD, every combining mark riding on a Latin letter dropped (accents, and also variation selectors and overlays that draw the same letter), `ß`/`æ`/`œ`/`ø` spelled out, invisible characters removed — format characters and every other default-ignorable code point, the Hangul fillers among them — so one typed into a word does not split it, and anything that is not a letter, digit or (non-Latin) mark ends a word — apostrophes and hyphens included. Another script's marks are kept, because there they are letters. An entry of several words matches as those words in that order. No substrings ("Scunthorpe" passes), no wildcards, and no folding of spelling — a digit or a look-alike letter from another alphabet makes a different word — so every inflection and compound a speaker uses is its own entry. Matching 1 000 sentence-length answers takes about a millisecond, so verdicts are not cached.
- **Evaluated when a tally is read, not stored on the answer.** The filter applies to answers already given: switched on mid-session it takes the offending line off the wall at once; switched off it puts every withheld answer back. A write that moves the list in force — switching the filter, picking a list, or moving the deck's `language` while the filter follows it — re-sends every free-text slide's recounted tally on `results.updated`, in parallel, logging rather than failing the save if one recount does; an editor Save that leaves the list where it was re-sends nothing. The exports' marks are therefore the filter as of the export, like every setting they report.
- **Which slides.** Word cloud, open-ended and typed quiz answers (`slideAnswersAreFiltered()` in `server/schemas.ts`) — the three whose answers reach a screen as words somebody wrote. A form's rows never reach the room (REQ061), so there is nothing to withhold there and its workbook rows carry no mark. Q&A, chat and participant names are out of scope.
- **What "withheld" means.** The whole answer, never a masked word. It is in no word, no response, no typed-answer group and not in the editor's REQ027 per-answer list (that list is drawn on the projector); an open-ended response's id goes with it. `totalVotes` still counts it and a public `filteredCount` says how many were withheld — a count names nobody — and the shared screen and every phone state it under the tally ("Answers withheld by the profanity filter: 2", in the deck's language on a phone) rather than silently drawing fewer answers than arrived. A withheld typed quiz answer is still scored and still counted in `respondentCount` and `scoring`: the filter decides what the screen shows, never who was right.
- **The projected screen receives nothing withheld — the presenter's own poll included.** The filter runs in `aggregateSlideResults()`, before any payload is built, so the public reads, the room-wide broadcast, the preview, segmented breakdowns and the presenter's credentialed poll all leave it out. The raw values travel only on a read that asks: `GET …/results` and `…/results/:slideId` with `?filtered=include` add `filteredAnswers` (id, text as written, createdAt — never a participant id) for a caller who can read the deck's authoring — the owner, the edit token, a collaborator at any level, exactly the readers of a word cloud's per-answer list — and `null` for everybody else, a results-link holder included. The results surface (`/results/:id`) is the one client read that asks; it shows the count and keeps the words behind a disclosure that starts closed, each marked "Filtered", since that page too may end up on a projector.
- **Exports.** The workbook marks every Responses row in a `Filtered` column (`true` beside the raw words, `false` for an answer the filter read and let through, empty where no filter read it), counts `Withheld by profanity filter` per slide on the Aggregates sheet while the deck filters, and states the switch and the list in force on the Summary. The PDF prints the withheld answers as written under "Withheld from the room by the profanity filter", after the tally the room saw.
- **One echo is deliberately left.** A participant's own scorecard still names the typed answer they gave: it is their own device reading its own record, not the room being shown somebody's words.
- **No screen deletes a withheld answer.** The presenter's moderation list (REQ027) leaves withheld answers out because it is drawn on the projector, and the results page carries no controls (REQ098), so taking one down for good is the REQ027 route with the id `filteredAnswers` names. An answer that is withheld and not deleted comes back on the wall if the filter is switched off — the settings panel says so beside the switch. A delete control for withheld answers is a candidate follow-up, not part of this slice.
- **Out of scope**, as briefed: the Q&A layer and the chat, custom per-deck lists, and AI moderation.

**Known limits and follow-up, from the pre-merge review (2026-10-25).** None blocks this record; each is recorded so it is not rediscovered.

- **Ambiguous words pass.** The lists hold only words with no innocent everyday meaning, as each list's header says; a word that is also an ordinary word passes even when it is meant as an insult (en `chink`, `fag`, `retard`, `honky`; pt `puto`; es `zorra`; fr `bâtard`). The filter is opt-in, matches lists and knows no context, so a false hold on harmless text costs more than an ambiguous word let through. Rules that read context are follow-up.
- **A known inconsistency in the en list.** "a bastard sword" and "the bitch had six puppies" are held, although both are innocent: en `bastard` stayed while fr `bâtard` went. Which of these is safely harmless is for a follow-up pass through the en list, not for a fix made in passing.
