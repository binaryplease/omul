## Description

As a presenter running a question in a large room, I want the shared screen and every phone in it to keep up while the room answers together, so that the cost of a question is set by how long it runs rather than by how many people are in it.

## Notes

Measured, not estimated, while fixing the same shape of problem on the leaderboard (REQ059). Not a defect — nothing here is wrong, and no number the room reads is stale. It is the remaining cost on the same path, and it is now the dominant one.

**What happens.** Every vote that lands ends in `broadcastResults`, which re-aggregates the slide and pushes `results.updated` to every socket joined to the presentation. That is one re-aggregation and one full-room fan-out per *individual answer*. On a room of 300, one question is 300 re-aggregations and 90,000 socket writes.

**Why it is filed now.** The deck-wide standings recount used to sit on this same path and cost the same way. Coalescing it (`server/coalesce.ts`) took a 300-answer question from 300 board recounts and 90,000 board socket writes down to 2 and 600. The measurement below is from that work, on a 20-question deck with one leaderboard slide and 300 answers arriving together:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| board recounts | 300 | 2 |
| board socket writes | 90,000 | 600 |
| **slide-tally socket writes** | **90,000** | **90,000** |
| total socket writes | 180,000 | 90,600 |

The slide's own tally is untouched by that fix and is now essentially the whole remaining cost of a large room answering a question.

**Why it is P2 and not P1.** A slide tally is a different thing from a deck-wide aggregate in one way that matters: it is the direct feedback for the gesture the participant just made, and a word cloud filling in word by word is part of what the product is. Anything done here has to keep that. The leaderboard had no such claim — nobody reads a scoreboard at 300 frames per question — which is why coalescing it was uncontroversial and coalescing this is not.

**Scope, when it is picked up.** The mechanism already exists and is tested: `createCoalescer` in `server/coalesce.ts` takes a key and a window. The open questions are product ones, not mechanical:

- Whether the window differs by slide type — a word cloud arguably wants every frame, a multiple-choice bar chart does not.
- Whether a coalesced tally should ride a shorter window than the leaderboard's 500 ms, since it is feedback rather than a summary.
- Whether the re-aggregation and the fan-out should be separated, so the tally can be recomputed at answer rate and *sent* at frame rate.

Out of scope: the standings recount, which is done (REQ059); the per-answer store writes, which are one row each and are not the cost here.

## Updates

- 2026-08-07 — Filed from the measurement taken while coalescing the standings recount (REQ059). The slide's own tally re-aggregates and fans out to the whole room once per individual answer: 300 answers on a 300-person room is 90,000 socket writes, which after the REQ059 fix is essentially the entire remaining cost of a large room answering one question. Left pending rather than fixed in the same change because a slide tally is live feedback for the gesture just made, so what may be merged is a product judgement the leaderboard did not require.
- 2026-09-04 — Fixed. The slide tally now rides `createCoalescer` on a **100 ms** window, keyed per (presentation, slide) so two slides of one deck never throttle each other. The three questions REQ150 left open, and how they were settled: **(1) One window for every slide type.** The type with the strongest claim to every frame is the word cloud, and 100 ms already honours it; no other type needs more, and a slower window for a bar chart would buy a rounding error on a burst that lasts seconds while adding a second cadence to reason about on the one path that publishes to a whole room. **(2) A fifth of the leaderboard's 500 ms, and the difference is the point.** A board is a summary read between questions; a slide tally is the direct feedback for the gesture just made, so the bound is set from the participant's side — 100 ms is the threshold under which a response still reads as instantaneous, which makes it the largest window that costs the feedback nothing. **(3) Re-aggregation folded with the fan-out, not separated from it.** Recomputing at answer rate would keep half the measured cost (300 full-slide rescans per question) and buy nothing: the coalescer re-reads the store at send time, so what goes out is already the current tally. What the word-cloud claim actually needed is preserved by the leading run rather than by the window — a room answering slower than ten times a second still gets exactly one frame per answer, byte for byte as before, which is every small room and the tail of every large one. Frames merge only above that rate, where word-by-word was never something a person could follow. Measured on the 40-seat burst in `server/ws.integration.test.ts`: 40 answers, 2 tally frames. The room-wide read is still made **without** `canEdit` — coalescing changed when a frame is sent and never what it reads — and a new test asserts every frame of a burst on a withholding deck carries the `withheld` marker and no numbers (REQ015–REQ017).
