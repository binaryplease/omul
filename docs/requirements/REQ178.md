## Description

As a reader of any surface this product puts in front of somebody — the app chrome, a slide on the projector, the same slide on a phone, a transactional email — I want every one of them set in the same sans typeface, Figtree, bundled with the build and resolving with no network, so that the product reads as one product and looks the same in a room with no internet as it does in a room with one.

## Notes

**What moves and what does not.** The sans face moves: `Sora` out, `Figtree` in, everywhere the product's own typography resolves. The **monospace face does not** — `DM Mono` is untouched, in the chrome, in the deck vocabulary's `mono` entry and in the mail's label chrome. Nothing else moves either: the palette, the type scale, the spacing, the icons and the drawn `BrandMark` are all out of scope, and the mark is drawn art that is never re-set in a typeface at all (REQ167).

**The author-facing vocabulary keeps its shape** (REQ092). `DECK_FONT_IDS` stays four ids — one bundled house face plus the three generics an author can pick instead — and the picker still offers all four. What changed is which face the house entry names; the feature is untouched.

**Why the stored id is the hard part.** The face is not only CSS here: a deck stores the id of the face it chose, and the set is *closed* — `DeckFontIdEnum` gates it at the schema, and the docstore re-parses every document on every read. Deleting `sora` from the enum would therefore not have degraded a deck's typography, it would have stopped the deck opening at all: a stored `sora` would fail `StoredPresentationSchema` and the read would throw. That is a worse failure than any typeface question, and it is why the id is **folded** rather than dropped — `RETIRED_DECK_FONT_IDS` maps `sora` to `figtree` inside the field's own schema, so a document written before the swap parses, reads back as `figtree`, renders in the house face on every surface, and shows the organizer a valid selection in the picker. A client still sending the old id is accepted for the same reason rather than answered with a 4xx.

**The mail is the surface that cannot be bundled to.** The client's faces ship as `.woff2` inside `dist/client/` and resolve offline; an email carries no bundle, so the only way to put a face in an inbox is a stylesheet the reader's mail client fetches from a font host when the mail is opened. That is a third-party runtime asset dependency for first-party chrome, which ADR-0016 forbids, and it tells the host who opened our mail and when. The mail shell had exactly such an `@import` from a public font host; it is removed rather than repointed at the new face. What the mail keeps is the brand faces named *first* in its stacks, for the reader who happens to have them installed, over the platform fallbacks that were already carrying the render on almost every client. The brand in an inbox is the card, the masthead and the accent, not the face.

**Done when.** No user-facing surface resolves to Sora and the dependency is gone; Figtree ships bundled and resolves with no network; the licensing record (`NOTICE.md`, `public/licenses/OFL-1.1-fonts.txt`) names what actually ships; a deck stored under the retired id opens and renders correctly in both colour schemes; and the repo's gate, test and build tasks (all named in `AGENTS.md`) stay green.
