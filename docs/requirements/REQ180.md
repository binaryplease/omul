## Description

As someone who deletes their account, I want that deletion to also resolve the presentations that ran under it — and the participant records keyed to those presentations — so that exercising a right of erasure does not depend on remembering to delete every deck first, and does not leave decks behind in a state that no account, no edit token and no support action can reach again.

## Notes

**What happens today.** Self-serve account deletion is Better Auth's own `deleteUser`, enabled at `server/accounts.ts:244` and mounted as `POST /api/auth/delete-user`; the UI gate in front of it is `DeleteAccountForm` (`src/auth.tsx:787`), which requires a typed confirmation phrase plus the account password before calling `deleteUser({ password })` (`src/auth.tsx:806`). Nothing is registered to run alongside it: there is no `databaseHooks` block in `server/`, and no `beforeDelete`/`afterDelete` is configured on the `deleteUser` options, so the route's own hook points are unused. What it erases is narrower than the account's footprint even inside the auth database. `internalAdapter.deleteUser` removes the `session`, `account` and `user` rows and nothing else (`better-auth@1.6.23`, `dist/db/internal-adapter.mjs`, `deleteUser`), with a `deleteUserSessions` call behind it on the same path (`dist/api/routes/update-user.mjs`). Two kinds of row survive it. `verification` entries are keyed by `identifier`, not by `userId`, so nothing collects them. Personal API keys survive outright: the plugin stores the owner as a plain indexed `referenceId` string with no foreign key and no cascade (`@better-auth/api-key@1.6.23`, `dist/index.mjs`, the `apikey` schema), and it registers no user-delete hook. An orphaned key fails closed rather than open — the key's session hook cannot resolve the user and throws `UNAUTHORIZED` — so it is residue rather than a live credential, but "the deletion removes your access keys" is not what the code does today, and that sentence is one a published deletion clause may be resting on.

**Why the presentations are untouched by it.** The domain data is in a different database entirely: the auth DB is deliberately kept as a separate SQLite file beside the zodstore docstore (`server/accounts.ts:39`), and nothing hangs an account deletion on the docstore. Deleting a *deck* does cascade — `deletePresentation` sweeps the room's records, the access grants and the slide comments before removing the deck (`server/services/presentations.ts:1071`, REQ146/REQ074) — but nothing invokes a deck delete when an account goes, so none of that cascade runs.

**What is left behind, and why nothing reaches it again.** A deck stores its owner as `creatorId` (`server/schemas.ts:6170`), indexed as a lookup key (`server/services/presentations.ts:148`), and every ownership decision is a comparison of that field against the user id resolved from the caller's session or API key — `if (!ownerId || ownerId !== userId)` (`server/routes/presentations.ts:371`). Once the `user` row is gone, no session can ever resolve to that id again, so the decks survive carrying an owner id that authorizes nobody. With them survive the six collections a deck delete would have swept through `eraseParticipantRecords` (`server/services/presentations.ts:1036`): the four REQ146 enumerates — `votes`, `responseVotes`, `qaQuestions`, `qaUpvotes` — plus `chatMessages` (REQ078) and the per-deck participant display names (REQ076), which hold a self-chosen `name` beside the `participantId` it belongs to. All six are keyed by `presentationId` and reached only through the deck that carries them.

**This is personal data, and the residue is unreachable rather than merely stale.** What stays is not aggregate: free text a participant typed into a word cloud or an open question, the questions they asked in the room, and the `participantId` tying those rows to one device across a session. Deleting an account is the moment at which a person most plausibly expects their data to be resolved, and it is also the moment after which no one — the former owner, an administrator, or an operator answering a written request — has any surface that resolves those decks. The admin surface is `POST /api/admin/actions` (`server/routes/admin.ts:61`), whose whole catalog is `reassign-presentation` and no purge, and a reassign takes a `presentationId` (`server/routes/admin.ts:57`) — the deck id, which the person who could have supplied it no longer has any screen to read.

**What this is not.** REQ146 (done) is *presentation* deletion: it makes deleting one deck erase the participant rows keyed to it. It is the reason a deck deleted *before* the account is clean, and it does nothing when the account goes first, because nothing invokes a deck delete. REQ140 (pending) is an automatic retention window at workspace level; a time-based sweep is not an answer to an erasure request, and it does not act on the personally-owned decks this requirement is about. Neither covers the gap.

**Done when.** Deleting an account leaves no presentation — and no participant record keyed to one — that names the deleted account as its owner and that no account, edit token or operator action can reach. The repository's gate (`mise run check`) and its test suite, as `AGENTS.md` declares them, apply unchanged.

**The decision this requirement does not make.** Which resolution is correct is open and is the product owner's to settle, not the builder's: the presentations could be deleted with the account, offered for deletion during the flow and deleted on confirmation, transferred to a workspace that outlives the account, or stripped of their owner and anonymized. Each has a different consequence for a co-presenter, a workspace and an audience holding a results link, and the wording the legal texts will need depends on which one is chosen. Settle this before building.

**A follow-up outside this repository, once this is built.** The published privacy policy currently documents the gap as the state of the product and will overstate the deletion the moment this lands, so the legal texts must be corrected in the same change window. As of the review of 2026-09-18 the passages are: in the private legal-documentation repository that is the source of truth for the published legal texts, the German privacy policy (`legal/datenschutz-de.md`) at the clause on deleting an account and what it does not remove (§5.2 on that date), the storage-duration table (§8), and the clause on the right of erasure that today directs a data subject to delete presentations first and then to write in about whatever is left (§9.3); the English twin (`legal/privacy-en.md`) at the matching clauses; and the deletion-concept annexe to the processing agreement (`legal/avv-anlage-3-loeschkonzept.md`). The marketing site's content tree carries downstream copies of the German and English policies (`app/content/legal/datenschutz.de.md`, `app/content/legal/privacy.en.md`) which are pulled along after the source is changed. Clause numbers are cited by date because that text is under revision — match on the subject described here rather than on the number.

**Known, and deliberately outside that done-when.** Three docstore collections name an account without being owned by one, so they survive a deletion and are not resolved by anything above: `StoredSlideComment.authorId` (`server/schemas.ts:6381`), the deleted person's own text on decks that are not theirs; `StoredDeckCollaborator.userId` / `.invitedBy` (`server/schemas.ts:6344`, `:6351`); and `StoredWorkspaceMember.userId` / `.invitedBy` (`server/schemas.ts:6273`, `:6276`). They are named here because whoever rewrites the erasure clause off this requirement needs the whole picture, and because a later requirement will have to take them — not because this one does.

**Out of scope.** The three account-referencing collections just named; the retention window (REQ140); any participant-facing "erase my answers" request, which is a different data subject making a different request; deletion of workspace-owned decks, which have no `creatorId` and outlive any single member by design; and the wording of the legal texts themselves, which is named above as the follow-up and is not edited from this repository.
